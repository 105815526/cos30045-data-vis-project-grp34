Hospitalised Injuries from Road Crashes in Australia (2011–2021)

Project By Dylan Ting Shu Han 105815322 Stanley Sia Ming Qian 105815526 Lee Kok Kiong 104399870

Road traffic crashes impose a severe and ongoing burden on the Australian healthcare system. While road fatalities frequently dominate media headlines and policy debates, non-fatal hospitalised injuries represent a substantially larger clinical and economic challenge—resulting in tens of thousands of annual admissions, prolonged inpatient bed stays, and long-term rehabilitation needs.

Drawing on official open-access datasets compiled by the Bureau of Infrastructure and Transport Research Economics (BITRE) from 2011 to 2021, this interactive data story addresses key usability and transparency limitations in standard government dashboards. Specifically, our visualisations highlight structural reporting discontinuities in Victoria (2012) and New South Wales (2017), contrast absolute volume changes against relative percentage shifts across road user groups, evaluate cumulative hospital bed-day strain, and examine regional and demographic severity patterns to support evidence-based healthcare resource allocation.
